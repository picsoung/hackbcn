Mental load is being the one who knows the list exists. OffLoad lifts it off that person and shares it out.

Mia, the agent, goes over the day before anyone looks at it, and a single question orders everything she does: does this change anyone's plans? When everything stays as it is, she handles it herself and says so afterwards. When someone has to move their day, she prepares the whole thing and asks for a yes or a no: one card, two buttons. And when it needs talking through, she opens a video call. That happens rarely, and the rarity is the measure of getting it right.

The technical decision behind it: the model interprets, the workflow decides. Times and distances are computed by deterministic code, which is where certainty lives. The model gets what it wins at: understanding a sentence said out loud, and choosing the right person to ask. Two of the workflow's eight steps pass through a model, and the step that decides the most is settled in code.

Every write to a calendar goes through a person's confirmation.
