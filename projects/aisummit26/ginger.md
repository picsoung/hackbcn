Wildfire teams already have enormous amounts of data: satellites, weather stations, terrain maps, LiDAR and infrastructure databases. The problem is turning all of it into one clear picture of what matters now, and what could happen next.

We built Ginger, a wildfire intelligence platform that connects prevention, simulation and response in one system.

Prevent combines live weather, satellite observations, terrain and vegetation to identify areas where conditions are becoming particularly receptive to fire and, importantly, explains why. Ginger processes Sentinel-2 imagery into vegetation indicators such as NDVI and NBR, while LiDAR data lets us reconstruct real forests in 3D, down to individual tree crowns and heights.

From there, Sage lets teams place a hypothetical ignition and simulate what could happen next. It combines terrain, fuels, moisture and weather, runs multiple wind and moisture scenarios, and tracks when the simulated fire reaches mapped trees, buildings, facilities and roads. For deeper modelling, we integrate terrain-resolved WindNinja wind fields with ELMFIRE fire propagation.

Finally, Ash turns all of that complexity into a conversational interface: teams can ask what is exposed, why, and which assumptions matter. AshConnect extends Ginger beyond the operations room, letting homes, schools and hospitals connect a location and choose how they want to receive future alerts.

Ginger turns data from hundreds of kilometres above Earth into intelligence about individual trees and the places beside them, helping people understand where conditions are dangerous, what could happen next, and why.
