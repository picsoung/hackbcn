Problem: Duolingo and similar apps rely on rote drilling with no context. It's boring and works poorly: people finish lessons but can't use the language in real situations.

What we built: A library of Spanish story quests, matched to the learner's interests (climbing, work, everyday life, family play) and level. Everything in-game is in Spanish, with voice characters you can answer aloud.

How it works: Following Wittgenstein's idea that meaning comes from use, a picture never translates a phrase and each word appears in at least two situations. A wrong choice isn't punished: the character's reaction reveals the meaning, and you retry in one click. Plots follow Blake Snyder's Save the Cat structure, so a goal and stakes make you speak because the hero needs it, not because an exercise says so. Characters speak through text-to-speech, and speech recognition checks your spoken answers.

Why it works: Context and a story keep you engaged, and active guessing with feedback beats passive memorizing (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Bjork).
