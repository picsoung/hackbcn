In 1900, Spain barely burned — 18 million rural people kept the land grazed, farmed and firewood-hungry. One generation of rural exodus, three million hectares of unmaintained plantations and fifty years of total suppression later, the fuel had nowhere to go but up: the regime flipped around 1972, and the three largest fires in Spain's entire record all burned in the last fourteen months.

The data to prevent this is public — satellites, weather models, infrastructure maps, population grids. Nobody had fused it into a decision.

FUSION is that decision layer. A god's-eye terminal with three views: HISTORIC replays 1900–2026 week by week, with every documented fire leaving a scar that fades on the real 13-year re-burn cycle — overlapping scars literally draw the risk map. LIVE crosses combustion physics (biomass × weather × dryness) with a criticality cascade — a substation is worth the hospitals that go dark when it burns — and lets a planner pour a budget onto the map: capped instruments, real costs (€90/ha goats to €1,200/ha machines), workforce in shepherds and burn crews. PREDICTION shows the same decision under three seasons: people exposed, people saved — in green.

Every number on screen is a public measurement or a labeled assumption. Prevention is the cheapest fire you'll never fight.
